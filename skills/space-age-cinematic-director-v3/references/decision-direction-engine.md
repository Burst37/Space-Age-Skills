# Frontier Decision and Direction Engine v4.0

This layer governs why a shot is chosen before prompt language is written.

## Director hierarchy
Audience information state → dramatic beat → point of view → information priority → temporal beat → editorial function → blocking → camera station/height → framing → movement or stillness → sensor/lens/focus → lighting/color → model execution.

Never reverse this hierarchy merely to use premium gear or a fashionable move.

## Shot necessity
Before moving camera determine what new information the end frame contains, why the audience must travel to learn it, whether blocking inside a locked frame would be stronger, and whether movement expresses a change in attention, geography, emotion, scale, power or relationship. If movement has no job, lock the camera.

## Viewpoint
Eye level: parity/observation. Low: scale/power when motivated. High: vulnerability/system/geography. Behind: pursuit/entry/withheld face. OTS: relationship/eyeline. POV: subjective information. Profile: geometry/distance. Frontal: confrontation/iconography. Overhead: pattern/system/geography. Context can reverse these meanings; avoid angle-emotion clichés.

## Movement decision
Reveal depth/geography → dolly, truck, crane, orbit, track.
Redirect attention without station change → pan/tilt.
Change magnification/compression from fixed station → zoom.
Accompany subject → track/follow/lead.
Pin subject while world moves → Snorri/locked-on.
Repeatable product choreography → robotic arm/motion control.
Vertical/geographic reveal → crane/Technocrane/drone.
Obstacle traversal → FPV with path physics.
Tension through refusal to move → locked-off.

## Frame A / Frame B contract
Design start and end before the path. Frame A states shot size, placement, depth planes and withheld information. Frame B states new information and payoff. Then solve a physically plausible path. The move ends on the dramatic beat, not an arbitrary clock.


## Root capture decision
For premium cinematic work, initialize camera selection at **Blackmagic URSA Cine 17K 65**. Treat its 65mm/17K capture baseline as the fidelity root, not one candidate among equals.

Run an exception test before changing bodies. A replacement must solve a shot requirement the root camera does not solve as well: required high-speed range, motion/shutter behavior, physical size/placement, specialty capture, mount/lens constraint, low-light/production requirement, or a deliberately required alternate capture/color workflow. Record the reason internally. If no concrete advantage exists, retain URSA Cine 17K 65.

After camera selection, propagate compatibility downstream rather than independently choosing prestige tokens:
**camera → sensor/format → legal image-circle/mount lenses → focal/focus behavior → rig/support → exposure/shutter → native capture/color vocabulary → finishing path → generator-facing meta tokens.**

Meta tokens are therefore consequences of the shot and capture decision. Reject conflicting capture identities and redundant token stacks.

## Lens and focus
Wide = proximity/environment/foreground exaggeration. Normal = natural premium perspective. Tele = isolation/compression/graphic layering. Macro/probe = material detail. Anamorphic = motivated horizontal/flare/bokeh character. Spherical = cleaner geometry. Aperture follows focus requirement rather than reflexive maximum blur. Check image circle, mount and close focus.
Choose deep, zone, tracked, rack, split-diopter or macro-plane focus deliberately.

## Blocking first
Define entrances/exits, marks, gaze, gesture peak, crossings/occlusion, foreground interaction, relationship change and settle. Camera does not rescue weak blocking. Reduce simultaneous AI actions until the important beat is unmistakable.

## Lighting direction
Define motivation, direction, size/quality, contrast, negative fill, separation, practicals, atmosphere, exposure priority and how the setup survives camera travel. Static sources should change facial ratios naturally as geometry changes; do not relocate the light source without motivation.

## Editorial coverage
One job per shot: MASTER geography; TWO-SHOT relationship; MCU/CU thought; INSERT fact; GESTURE action; POV information; MOVING REVEAL discovery; HERO payoff. Duplicate coverage only when meaning changes.

## Sequence energy
Assign internal energy 1–5 from camera velocity, subject motion, cut frequency, focal extremity and scale change. Shape a build, release, pulse or crescendo. Maximum energy everywhere is not direction.

## Compatibility matrix
Classify combinations preferred, compatible, conditional or conflict across narrative purpose, path physics, rig, lens, sensor coverage, focus, blocking, lighting continuity, parallax, duration, transition and model risk.

Preferred examples: translation + foreground layers + wide/normal; Technocrane + moderate wide for geography-to-character; robotic arm + macro/normal for product; tele tracking + vehicle/remote support; 65mm + compatible large-format glass; probe/macro + slider/robot.

Conflict examples: extreme tele + tight close orbit without sufficient radius; macro + uncontrolled large handheld translation; long lens + tiny interior + large arc; insufficient lens image circle; URSA Cine 17K 65 + Leitz HUGO; locked-off + camera-generated parallax; handheld + robotic-repeatability language; seamless loop + irreversible event without reset; ultra-shallow DOF + chaotic blocking + complex move.

## Duration budget
4–8s: one primary camera move, one primary subject action, optional focus event.
8–15s: one developed move or two staged phases.
15–30s: compound choreography only with clear beat changes.
Do not compress a long oner into a short clip.

## Generative feasibility
Preserve directorial intent while simplifying execution when necessary. Reduce secondary environment motion, unnecessary subject motion, occlusion, rotation, focal change and path complexity before sacrificing the primary dramatic beat.

## Candidate selection
Create three internal candidates: robust, expressive, frontier/experimental. Reject contradictions. Select the candidate that communicates the beat with the least unnecessary complexity. Frontier does not mean busiest.

## Preflight
Check motivation, simpler alternatives, end-frame payoff, equipment compatibility, focus feasibility, lighting geometry, screen direction, target-model complexity, correct distinction between zoom/translation and pan/truck/orbit/roll, and whether physical behavior is described rather than hidden behind brand names. Redesign failures before delivery.
